Predict first — the overlap trap

Vikas and Manoj have crossed. How many students are strictly between them?

35 students · Vikas 22nd-L · Manoj 19th-R

How many sit strictly between Vikas and Manoj?

Working: L + R = 22 + 19 = 41 > 35 → overlap. Excess = 41 − 35 = 6. But that 6 includes Vikas and Manoj themselves, each counted twice → M = 6 − 2 = 4. Answer: 4.

The formulas, and where each comes from

Single person, both ends

N = L + R − 1

Subtracting 1 removes the person's own double-count.

Two persons, no overlap

M = N − (L + R)

Only when L + R ≤ N. People between are counted exactly once.

Two persons, overlapping

M = (L + R) − N − 2

The −2 removes the two reference persons, each double-counted.

Swap case

N = L₂ + R₁ − 1

After A moves to B's seat, A's new left rank and B's old right rank are from both ends of the *same* seat.

Solution

Exam shortcuts

Speed card for the exam hall. Read after Practice — then race the timed set.

  • Use

    N = L + R − 1

    One person: left-rank + right-rank − 1 = total.

  • Trap

    Overlap formula

    Between from opposite ends: M = L+R−N−2. If M < 0 or > min(L,R)−2 → impossible.

  • Check

    Between count

    |pos₁ − pos₂| − 1. Never the raw difference.

  • Speed

    After interchange

    New left of A = old left of the other. N still = newL + oldR − 1.
