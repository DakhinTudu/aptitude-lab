2. Predict Before You Reveal

Avoid the #1 exam trap: Double-counting in overlapping cross-overs.

Challenge Case: The Overlapping Row

In a row of 35 students, Vikas is 22nd from the left and Manoj is 19th from the right.
How many students sit strictly between Vikas and Manoj?

Step-by-Step Resolution:

Correct Answer: 4 students.

1. Sum of given ranks: $L + R = 22 + 19 = 41$.
2. Notice that $L + R (41) > \text{Total } N (35)$. This immediately indicates an Overlapping (Cross-Over) Case where Vikas and Manoj have crossed past each other.
3. In the overlapping sum $(22 + 19)$, who has been counted twice?
• The students sitting between them ($M$) are counted from both left and right.
• Vikas himself is counted twice (once in his 22, once when counting Manoj from right).
• Manoj himself is counted twice (once in his 19, once when counting Vikas from left).
4. Therefore, total excess $= M + 2$.

M = (L + R) - N - 2 = 41 - 35 - 2 = 4

Why 6 is the trap: Subtracting 35 from 41 gives 6, which forgets to subtract the 2 reference persons themselves!

3. Core Ranking Axioms & Formula Matrix

Precise equations that guarantee zero off-by-one errors in competitive tests.

1. Single Person from Both Ends

When one individual's rank is known from both terminals:

N = L + R - 1

Rank from other end: $R = N - L + 1$ or $L = N - R + 1$. The $+1$ prevents omitting the person.

2. Overlapping vs. Non-Overlapping

Compare $(L_A + R_B)$ with total $N$:

  • Simple ($N \ge L_A + R_B$): $M = N - (L_A + R_B)$
  • Overlapping ($N < L_A + R_B$): $M = (L_A + R_B) - N - 2$
  • Feasibility Check: Overlap is only physically possible if $M \le \min(L_A, R_B) - 2$.

3. Position Interchange Rule

A is $L_1$ from left; B is $R_1$ from right. When they swap, A is now $L_2$ from left:

Total N = L₂ + R₁ - 1

B's new rank from right $= R_1 + (L_2 - L_1)$. Both persons shift by the exact same distance!

4. Maximum vs. Minimum Row Capacity

When $L_A, R_B$, and between count $M$ are given without total $N$:

  • Maximum Capacity: $N_{\max} = L_A + R_B + M$
  • Minimum Capacity: $N_{\min} = L_A + R_B - M - 2$ (if feasible)
