2. Predict Before You Reveal

Spot the trap: part-to-part vs. part-to-whole is the #1 ratio error in banking exams.

Challenge: Coin Denominations

A bag contains ₹1, 50 paise, and 25 paise coins in the ratio 3 : 4 : 8. The total value of the bag is ₹420.
How many ₹1 coins are there?

Trap option: 420 × 3/15 = 84 coins  |  Correct approach: Weight denominations first.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Correct Answer: 180 coins.

Count ratio $= 3 : 4 : 8$. Let there be $3k$, $4k$, $8k$ coins of ₹1, 50p, 25p respectively.
Value equation (in rupees): $$3k \times 1 + 4k \times 0.50 + 8k \times 0.25 = 420$$ $$3k + 2k + 2k = 420 \implies 7k = 420 \implies k = 60.$$ Number of ₹1 coins $= 3k = 3 \times 60 = \mathbf{180}$.

Why "84" is wrong: Dividing ₹420 by 15 treats all ratios as if the coins have equal denominations. A 50p coin contributes half a rupee and a 25p coin contributes a quarter — you must weight them.

3. Core Ratio Rules & Exam Traps

Fast-reference formulas that eliminate every standard trap.

1. Part-to-Whole Conversion

Ratio $a : b \implies$ Part A fraction $= \dfrac{a}{a+b}$, Part B fraction $= \dfrac{b}{a+b}$.

⚠ Never write Milk fraction as $\dfrac{a}{b}$ — that's part-to-part.

2. Chained Ratio (A:B and B:C)

$$A:B:C = (a \cdot c) : (b \cdot c) : (b \cdot d)$$ where $A:B = a:b$ and $B:C = c:d$.

LCM-bridge: make the common term ($B$) equal before combining.

3. Proportional Division

Divide $T$ in ratio $a:b:c$:
Unit value $u = T/(a+b+c)$
Shares $= a \cdot u,\; b \cdot u,\; c \cdot u$

4. Mean / Third / Fourth Proportional

Mean of $a, b$: $\;x = \sqrt{ab}$
Third of $a, b$: $\;x = b^2/a$
Fourth of $a, b, c$: $\;x = bc/a$

5. Adding a Constant to Ratio Terms

Numbers in ratio $a:b$ ⟹ actual values $ax, bx$.
If constant $p$ added: new ratio $c:d$, solve: $$x = \frac{dp - cp}{ad - bc} \quad \text{(signed)}$$

⚠ Adding to both terms changes the ratio unless $a = b$.

6. Direct vs. Inverse Variation

Direct: $\dfrac{y_1}{x_1} = \dfrac{y_2}{x_2}$ (both increase together).
Inverse: $x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2$ (one increases, other decreases).
