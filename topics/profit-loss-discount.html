Profit, Loss & Marked Price

Three prices, three different bases. Markup % on CP. Discount % on MP. Profit % on CP. Every trap comes from mixing them up. Path: Understand → Predict → calculators → Play labs → Practice → Shortcuts.

Understand: price waterfall

The bar grows by markup (on CP), then shrinks by discount (on MP). Where it lands relative to CP is the net profit or loss.

Reverse calculator — find the missing price

Given any two of CP, SP, MP, and P/L%, find the third. The most common exam question type after the basics.

Cost Price

Sold at 20% loss → CP = SP / (1 − 0.20) = 960 / 0.80 = ₹1,200.

Target profit calculator

A high-frequency exam question: "At what MP should I list so that after a given discount, I still earn a target profit?"

Required MP₹1,250
Required markup %56.25%
SP after discount₹1,000
Check: net profit25%
Derivation
SP must be CP × (1 + 25/100) = ₹1,000. MP must satisfy MP × (1 − 20/100) = ₹1,000 → MP = ₹1,000 / 0.80 = ₹1,250.
Master formula
MP = CP × (1 + P/100) / (1 − d/100)
Where P = target profit % (on CP) and d = discount % (on MP).

Play: successive discount & markup

Two successive increases (markups) also compound. Two decreases (discounts) also compound. Both use the same multiplication chain.

Equivalent single discount28%
Naive sum (wrong)30%
Final multiplier0.72×
Gap between them2%
Equivalent single markup32%
Naive sum (wrong)30%
Final multiplier1.32×
Gap between them−2%
Note: Successive markups increase faster than the naive sum — 20% then 10% gives 1.20 × 1.10 = 1.32 → 32% total markup, not 30%.

Equal SP with +x% and −x% — why always a loss

Don't memorise (x/10)². Two items, same SP: one bought cheap (gain), one bought dear (loss). Total CP > total SP every time.

Loss %1%
CP of gain item₹900
CP of loss item₹1,100
Formula(x/10)²

When two articles are sold at the same SP with ±x%, the total is always a loss of (x/10)²%. If they had the same CP, the answer would be 0%. The base swap is what creates the loss.

Dishonest shopkeeper simulator

The shopkeeper claims to sell at cost price, but uses a lighter weight. What is the real profit %?

True CP of goods sold₹90
Revenue charged₹100
Real profit₹10
Real profit %11.11%
Naive guess10%
Formula derivation
Gain % = (True Weight − False Weight) / False Weight × 100 = (1000 − 900) / 900 × 100 = 11.11%.
⚠️ Classic trap: The naive answer is 100/1000 = 10%. But the denominator must be the actual goods sold, not the claimed weight. That's why the answer is 100/900 = 11.11%.

Promotional offer simulator — Buy X Get Y Free

"Buy 4 get 1 free" is not the same as 20% off. Compare promotional schemes against a plain discount.

Total items received5
Amount paid₹400
Value received₹500
Effective discount %20%
With extra discount20%
Effective discount formula
Effective discount = Y / (X + Y) × 100 = 1 / 5 × 100 = 20%. You pay for 4 out of 5 items.
Buy 1 Get 1 Free
You pay for 1 of 2 → effective discount = 1/2 = 50%. Equivalent to a half-price sale.
Buy 2 Get 1 Free
Pay for 2 of 3 → effective = 1/3 = 33.33%. Better than 30% off but less than 35% off.
Buy 3 Get 2 Free
Pay for 3 of 5 → effective = 2/5 = 40%. Equivalent to 40% flat discount.
Trap alert
"Buy 3 get 1 free" is 25% off, not 33%. Always divide the free items by the total received.

How overall % is computed
Overall P/L% = (Total SP − Total CP) / Total CP × 100. Never average individual percentages — that ignores weights.
⚠️ Trap: A 20% gain on one item and 20% loss on another does NOT cancel out unless their CPs are equal. Always weight by CP amount.

Profit on SP vs profit on CP — the silent trap

"20% profit" almost always means on CP. But some problems say "profit is 20% of SP". These are not the same.

SP (if on CP)₹120
SP (if on SP)₹125
P% on CP (if on SP)25%
Formula (on SP)P%×100/(100−P%)
Conversion formula
If profit is P% of SP, then SP = CP / (1 − P/100). Profit on CP = P × 100 / (100 − P). Example: 20% on SP → 25% on CP.

Overheads & expenses — the real cost

Transport, packaging, labour, commission — all get added to CP before profit is computed. A classic exam trick.

Effective CP₹550
MP (on effective CP)₹660
SP₹627
Net profit ₹₹77
Net profit %14%
Trap: markup − discount15%
Key insight: Effective CP = Purchase price + expenses. Every markup and profit % must be computed on this effective CP, not just the purchase price. Otherwise the answer will be off.

Exam shortcuts

Speed card for the exam hall.

SP = CP × multiplier
Profit p% → ×(1+p/100). Loss → ×(1−p/100). Chain discounts the same way.
Successive discount
20% + 10% ≠ 30%. Do 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 → 28% off. Effective = d₁ + d₂ − d₁d₂/100.
Equal SP ±x%
Always a loss of (x/10)²%. Never add the two percentages.
False weight
Gain% = (true − false)/false × 100. Denominator is false weight, not true.
Buy X Get Y
Effective discount = Y/(X+Y) × 100. Buy 3 get 1 = 25%, not 33%.
Target MP
MP = CP × (1 + P/100) / (1 − d/100). Profit on CP, discount on MP.
Profit on SP
P% on SP → P × 100/(100−P) on CP. Example: 20% on SP = 25% on CP.
Overheads
Effective CP = Purchase + expenses. Apply all % on effective CP.
Multiple articles
Overall % = (ΣSP − ΣCP) / ΣCP × 100. Never average the individual percentages.
