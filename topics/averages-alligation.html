2. Predict Before You Reveal

Avoid the #1 Alligation Trap: Mixing Cost Prices with a Selling Price.

Challenge: The Commercial Profit Mixture

A grocer mixes two varieties of pulses costing ₹15/kg and ₹20/kg.
He sells the resulting mixture at ₹19.80/kg, earning a 10% profit.
In what ratio must the two varieties be mixed?

Mathematical Resolution:

Correct Answer: 2 : 3.

The Fundamental Law of Alligation: All three values entered into the alligation cross must belong to the same cost basis.
₹15 and ₹20 are Cost Prices. ₹19.80 is a Selling Price with a 10% profit margin included.
$$\text{Cost Price of Mixture} = \frac{\text{SP}}{1 + \text{Profit}\%} = \frac{19.80}{1.10} = ₹18\text{/kg}$$

Applying alligation with the true mean CP ($18$):
$$\text{Ratio} = (20 - 18) : (18 - 15) = 2 : 3$$ Why 1 : 24 is wrong: Using ₹19.80 treats the grocer's profit margin as if it were actual raw pulse cost!

3. Core Deduction Axioms & Exam Traps

Speed rules that turn 3-minute algebra into 15-second alligations.

1. Repeated Dilution Formula

From container with capacity $C$ of pure liquid, $x$ liters are drawn and replaced with water, repeated $n$ times:

Q_rem = C × (1 - x / C)ⁿ

Water present $= C - Q_{\text{rem}}$. Pure liquid fraction decreases geometrically.

2. Group Replacement Shift

When one member of weight $W_{\text{old}}$ is replaced by $W_{\text{new}}$ in a group of $N$ persons:

W_new = W_old + (N × Δ)

If average decreases, $\Delta$ is negative. No need to calculate the grand sum!

3. Speed-Time Alligation Base

When applying alligation to speeds ($S_1, S_2$) and average speed ($S_m$):

⚠ The resulting ratio is the ratio of TIMES taken, NOT distances!

Speed has units $\text{km}/\text{hour}$. Denominator is time, so alligation weights time.

4. Bowling Average Dynamics

$$\text{Bowling Average} = \frac{\text{Runs Conceded}}{\text{Wickets Taken}}$$

*Caution:* A "reduction" or "improvement" in bowling average means the number becomes smaller (e.g. 12.4 improving by 0.4 becomes 12.0). Alligation weights by wickets.
