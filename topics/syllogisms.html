2. Predict Before You Reveal

Test your grasp of the critical distinction between Definite Truth and Valid Possibility.

Challenge Case: Blocked Overlap Evaluation

Given Statements:
1. Some Doctors are Teachers.
2. No Teacher is an Artist.

Candidate Conclusion: “All Doctors being Artists is a possibility.”
Does this conclusion follow?

Mathematical Resolution:

Correct Answer: No, it does NOT follow.

Let $D$ = Doctors, $T$ = Teachers, $A$ = Artists.
Premise 1 states $D \cap T \neq \emptyset$ (there exists a non-empty subset of Doctors inside Teachers).
Premise 2 states $T \cap A = \emptyset$ (nothing in $T$ can ever enter $A$).
Therefore, the subset $(D \cap T)$ can never enter $A$.

Because at least some Doctors can never touch Artists, it is physically impossible for ALL Doctors to be Artists! (However, note that “Some Doctors being Artists is a possibility” WOULD be true!).

3. Core Deduction Axioms & Exam Traps

Rules that turn 2-minute guessing into 15-second deterministic deductions.

1. "Only a few" Proposition

"Only a few A are B" is a composite of two distinct truths:

  • Definite 1: Some A are B ($A \cap B \neq \emptyset$)
  • Definite 2: Some A are NOT B ($A \setminus B \neq \emptyset$)
  • Possibility Invariant: "All A being B is a possibility" is IMPOSSIBLE (False).
  • Direction Trap: "All B being A is a possibility" is VALID (True)!

2. The 3 "Either / Or" Conditions

Two conclusions form an Either-Or pair if and only if:

  1. Both conclusions are independently doubtful / false in the basic diagram.
  2. Both share the exact same subject and predicate.
  3. They form a valid complementary pair:
    • Some + No
    • All + Some not

*Caution:* All + No is NEVER Either-Or (both can be false simultaneously).

3. Definite vs. Possibility

• Definite conclusion: True in ALL valid Euler diagrams.
• Possibility: True in AT LEAST ONE valid diagram without violating given premises.
• Certainty Trap: If a statement is already 100% definitely true, stating it as a "possibility" does NOT follow in competitive exam grading.

4. Two Negatives Axiom

When two premises are both negative (e.g. No A is B and No B is C):

Zero definite relationship can ever be deduced between the extreme sets (A and C). Any definite statement ("No A is C", "All A are C") is doubtful!
