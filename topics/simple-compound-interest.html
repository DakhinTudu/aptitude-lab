Simple & Compound Interest

The gap between SI and CI is interest on interest. This lab covers 10 modules — growth curves, compounding frequency, instalments, split investments, reverse calculators, growth & depreciation, and 32 practice questions.

Why CI is bigger: interest on interest

Each year, CI earns interest on principal (same as SI) plus interest on earlier interest (unique to CI). The green portion is what makes the gap.

The gap at year T = the sum of all the green segments up to year T. The Δ formula for 2 years is P·(R/100)² — it's only the interest-on-interest block from year 2. For 3 years it adds another block from year 3, plus interest-on-interest-on-interest.

Key insight
More frequent compounding = more interest. Because each period's interest starts earning interest sooner. The continuous-compounding limit is P × e^(RT).

Fractional periods — 2.5 years convention

When the time has a fraction (like 2.5 years), the standard aptitude treatment is: CI for the whole years, then SI for the remaining fraction.

Amount after whole years₹12,100
SI for fraction₹605
Final amount₹12,705
Total CI₹2,705
If pure CI (log)₹12,702
Convention used
Amount after 2 years = P(1.10)² = ₹12,100. Then half-year SI = 12,100 × 10% × 0.5 = ₹605. Final = ₹12,705. Note: some contracts may use pure fractional CI; aptitude exams use this convention.
⚠️ Convention note: Most SSC/Banking/SEBI questions expect whole-year CI + SI on remaining fraction. Always check if the question specifies a different compounding rule.

Equal instalments — the present-value trap

A loan is repaid in equal annual instalments. Each future instalment is discounted to today, then summed to equal the loan.

Each instalment₹844.83
Total paid₹2,534.49
Total interest₹434.49
Present value sum₹2,100
Trap guess (loan/n)₹700
Formula & trap
Each instalment x paid 1, 2, 3... years later is worth x/(1+r)¹, x/(1+r)², ... today. Sum = Loan. The trap loan/n ignores time value of money — each rupee later is worth less than a rupee today.

Split investment solver

A sum is divided into two parts at different rates. Given the total interest, find the allocation.

₹5,000
₹5,000
Method
x·R₁·T/100 + (Total−x)·R₂·T/100 = TotalSI. Solve for x. Alternatively, use weighted average: overall rate = TotalSI×100 / (Total×T), then interpolate between R₁ and R₂.
⚠️ Trap: splitting at (Total/2, Total/2) is only correct if total SI equals the average of the two rates × P × T / 100 — check.

Doubling: exact vs Rule of 72

CI doubling time = ln 2 / ln(1+R). SI doubling = 100/R. The Rule of 72 (T ≈ 72/R) is a quick approximation — useful for exams, but not exact.

SI doubles in10.00 yr
CI doubles in (exact)7.27 yr
Rule of 727.20 yr
Error0.07 yr
CI wins by2.73 yr
SI doubling
T = 100/R. Exact always. At 10% → 10 years.
CI doubling
T = ln 2 / ln(1+R). At 10% → 7.27 years.
Rule of 72
T ≈ 72/R. Quick mental estimate. At 10% → 7.2 years. Close, not exact.
When to use which
Exact ln-formula for calculation questions. Rule of 72 for quick sanity checks in timed exams.

Exam shortcuts

Speed card for the exam hall.

CI − SI (2 yr)
Δ₂ = P(R/100)². For 3 yr: Δ₃ = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100) = Δ₂ × (3 + R/100).
More frequent = more money
Rate ÷ k, periods × k. Monthly > quarterly > half-yearly > annual.
Doubling shortcuts
SI: 100/R. CI: ln2/ln(1+R). Quick check: 72/R.
Successive rates
Multiply multipliers: (1+r₁)(1+r₂)(1+r₃). Never add rates.
Instalments
x = P·r·(1+r)ⁿ / [(1+r)ⁿ−1]. Never loan/n — that ignores time value.
Split investment
x·R₁·T/100 + (Total−x)·R₂·T/100 = TotalSI. Solve for x.
Depreciation
V = P(1−R/100)ⁿ. Same as CI but with multiplier < 1.
Effective annual rate
EAR = (1+R/k)ᵏ − 1. For 10% half-yearly → 10.25%.
Fractional period
Convention: CI for whole years + SI for fraction. Check if question specifies otherwise.
Reverse P from A
P = A/(1+R/100)ᵀ. Don't subtract interest and call it P.
