Simple & Compound Interest

The gap between SI and CI is not a formula — it's interest on interest. This page draws both curves, lets you slide a year-marker to read the gap at any time, and shows that the CI−SI difference is *literally* the compounding of earlier interest.

Drag the year slider. The gap at any year is *priced* on the chart — it's the difference between the green (CI) and blue (SI) curves at that point.

Why CI is bigger: interest on interest

At each year, CI earns interest on *principal* (same as SI) plus interest on *earlier interest* (unique to CI). The green portion is what makes the gap.

The gap at year T = the sum of all the green segments up to year T. That's why the Δ formula for 2 years is P·(R/100)² — it's *only* the interest-on-interest block from year 2. For 3 years it adds another block from year 3, plus interest-on-interest-on-interest. The formulas you memorise are just accounting for these extra compounding blocks.

Doubling: linear vs exponential

SI doubling time is `100/R`. CI doubling time is `ln 2 / ln(1+R)`. Slide the rate and watch them separate.

    SI% on CI stem

    Don't treat CI as simple %. Use (1+R/100)^n − 1.
