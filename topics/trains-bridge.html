Two-train crossing simulator

Both lengths matter, and relative speed is the key. Toggle direction to see why opposite directions add speeds while same direction subtracts them.

Relative speed (km/h)108
Relative speed (m/s)30
Total distance300
Cross time10 s
Formulav₁ + v₂
Why this works
Opposite directions: relative speed = v₁ + v₂. Same direction: |v₁ − v₂|. In both cases, the crossing distance is the sum of the two train lengths (L₁ + L₂).

Moving person simulator

The train crosses a walking man. Same direction → subtract. Opposite → add. But the distance is always just the train's own length.

Relative speed (km/h)48
Relative speed (m/s)13.33
Distance to cover150 m
Cross time11.25 s
Formula|v_t − v_p|
⚠️ Key trap: Some students add the person's length to the train length. The man is treated as a point — his length doesn't matter. Distance = train length only.

Unknown-length laboratory — two observations

Give the train two crossing observations. Solve for its speed and length. The trick: the difference in distances over the difference in times gives the speed.

Speed (m/s)15
Speed (km/h)54
Train length180 m
ΔDistance240 m
ΔTime16 s
Method
v = (d₂ − d₁) / (t₂ − t₁) = (240 − 0) / (28 − 12) = 15 m/s. Train length L = v×t₁ − d₁ = 15×12 − 0 = 180 m.
Two-observation shortcut
v = ΔDistance / ΔTime
Then L = v × t₁ − d₁ (or v × t₂ − d₂). Works for pole+platform, two platforms, two bridges, and any two crossing times.

Meeting-point simulator

Two trains start from different stations, moving toward each other. Relative speed = v₁ + v₂. Time to meet = distance / relative speed. Meeting point = station 1 + v₁ × time.

Relative speed100 km/h
Time to meet (from A)3 h
A travelled180 km
B travelled120 km
Distance ratio3 : 2
Key insight
Trains cover distance in ratio v₁ : v₂. Since they meet at the same time, distance travelled is proportional to speed. A travels d × v₁/(v₁+v₂); B travels d × v₂/(v₁+v₂).

Exam shortcuts

Speed card for the exam hall.

Distance = L_t + L_b
Cross time = (train + bridge) / speed. Pole/man: bridge = 0 → only L_t.
Never forget train length
Crossing a bridge is never "bridge ÷ speed" alone — tail must clear.
5/18 first
km/h → m/s: ×5/18. m/s → km/h: ×18/5. Do this before arithmetic.
Relative speed
Same direction: |v₁−v₂|. Opposite: v₁+v₂. Then (L₁+L₂)/v_rel.
Moving person
Distance = L_t only. Same direction: v_t − v_p. Opposite: v_t + v_p.
Two observations
v = ΔDistance / ΔTime. Then L = v×t₁ − d₁. Works for pole+platform, two platforms.
Meeting point
Time = d / (v₁+v₂). A's distance = d × v₁/(v₁+v₂). Ratio of distances = ratio of speeds.
Stationary train
Same as bridge: L₁ + L₂ distance, v = v_train.
Two trains overtaking
Same direction: (L₁+L₂)/(v₁−v₂). If equal lengths, 2L/(v₁−v₂).
