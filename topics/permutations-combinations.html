Counting principles — add or multiply?

Before formulas, students need to know: independent stages multiply, mutually exclusive alternatives add. This is the foundation of everything else.

Addition principle (OR)
If a task can be done in m ways OR n ways (mutually exclusive), total = m + n. Example: choose one dessert from 3 cakes or 4 ice-creams → 3 + 4 = 7.
Multiplication principle (AND)
If a task has m ways for stage 1 AND n ways for stage 2 (independent), total = m × n. Example: pick 1 shirt from 5 and 1 pant from 4 → 5 × 4 = 20.
Result
5 × 4 = 20
Stages AND → multiply. Pick one of 5 stage-1 options, then one of 4 stage-2 options = 5 × 4 = 20 outcomes.

Circular — why we fix one person

A round table has no "first seat". Rotating everyone looks the same. Fix one person to kill rotations — then arrange the rest.
