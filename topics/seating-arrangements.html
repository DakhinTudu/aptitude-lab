Linear & Circular Seating Arrangements

Master the geometric and directional mechanics of competitive reasoning puzzles: facing centre vs. facing outward, linear row perspectives (North vs. South), between-count rules, and systematic branch-and-prune deduction.

Orientation Invariant: For a person facing the centre of a circle, turning Left moves clockwise, and turning Right moves anti-clockwise. When facing outward, the orientations invert: Left is anti-clockwise and Right is clockwise.

Core Structural & Directional Rules

1. Circular Table Geometry ($N=8$)
  • Opposite Seat ($N$ even): Position $\text{Opp}(i) = (i + N/2) \pmod N$. For $N=8$, opposite is always 4 steps away.
  • Facing Centre: Left = Clockwise ($+1$), Right = Anti-Clockwise ($-1$).
  • Facing Outward: Left = Anti-Clockwise ($-1$), Right = Clockwise ($+1$).
  • Between Count: $k$-th to the left means $(k - 1)$ persons between them.
2. Linear Row Invariants ($0 \dots N-1$)
  • Facing North: Left = West (index $-1$), Right = East (index $+1$).
  • Facing South: Left = East (index $+1$), Right = West (index $-1$).
  • Between Count: $| \text{Index}_A - \text{Index}_B | - 1$.
  • Parallel Rows: Row 1 (South) faces Row 2 (North); right of Row 1 points West.
3. 4-Stage Human Deduction Protocol
  • 1. Anchor: Place the primary fixed/definite clue (e.g. extreme end or relative pair).
  • 2. Branch: If at most 2 possibilities exist, maintain Case 1 vs. Case 2.
  • 3. Prune: Apply negative constraints ("cannot sit next to A") to kill contradictions.
  • 4. Close: Place remaining individuals and verify all $M$ puzzle clues.
