2. Predict Before You Reveal

Avoid the #1 Table DI Trap: Confusing Absolute Increase with Percentage Increase.

Challenge: Growth Rate Comparison

From FY 2023 to FY 2024:
• Delta's revenue jumped from ₹330 Cr to ₹396 Cr (an increase of ₹66 Cr).
• Alpha's revenue jumped from ₹180 Cr to ₹225 Cr (an increase of ₹45 Cr).
Which company recorded the HIGHER percentage growth rate?

Mathematical Resolution:

Correct Answer: Alpha recorded higher percentage growth (25% vs 20%).

$$\text{Alpha's Growth} = \frac{225 - 180}{180} \times 100\% = \frac{45}{180} \times 100\% = \mathbf{25.0\%}$$ $$\text{Delta's Growth} = \frac{396 - 330}{330} \times 100\% = \frac{66}{330} \times 100\% = \mathbf{20.0\%}$$

The Exam Trap: Banking test writers constantly exploit candidates under time pressure who confuse highest absolute addition with highest relative rate. A smaller base makes a moderate addition represent a vastly larger percentage surge.

3. Fast DI Calculation Shortcuts & Axioms

Techniques that cut tabular calculation time by 60%.

1. The "Than" Anchor Rule

In questions asking "X is what % more/less than Y":

% Change = (|X - Y| / Y) × 100%

The entity immediately following the word "than" is ALWAYS the denominator.

2. Ratio of Averages Shortcut

When asked for the ratio of the average of A to the average of B over $k$ years:

(Total_A / k) : (Total_B / k) ≡ Total_A : Total_B

Never divide by $k$. Compute the ratio of the row/column totals directly.

3. Base-Split Approximation

To calculate complex fractions like $171 / 225$ without decimal division:

  • $100\% = 225$, so $50\% = 112.5$
  • $25\% = 56.25 \implies 75\% = 168.75$
  • Remaining $= 171 - 168.75 = 2.25$ ($1\% = 2.25$)
  • Result: $75\% + 1\% = 76.0\%$ exactly!

4. Unit & Denomination Traps

Always verify the table header scale (₹ Crore, Thousands, Millions).

In multi-table DI, one table is often given in Lakhs and another in Crores ($1\text{ Cr} = 100\text{ Lakhs}$). Never calculate cross-table sums without normalizing units.
